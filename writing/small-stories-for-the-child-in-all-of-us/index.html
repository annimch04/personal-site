Everything Was a Little Wild

The world knew her name.

Once upon a time there was a little girl who lived on a farm.

She rode horses, chased chickens, and sometimes ran from snakes by the spring-fed pond.

The cats were wild, the dogs were mutts, and no one ever brushed her hair.

She rose with the sun, bare feet in rubber boots, not to feed but to feel a world wake.

The horses would snort and stomp, impatient for her climb.

The chickens squawk annoyed at being chased yet still they never learned.

The pond was always cool and quiet, holder of secrets for the girl who never asked.

She didn’t mind the tangled hair.
Or mud on her knees.
Or the way the wind always seemed to know her name.

Because on that farm, everything was a little wild—
and so was she.

One Bark

He had already said what he meant.

The morning was quiet. Even the fences seemed asleep.

Atlas lay in his yard, watching the sidewalk.

Then someone turned the corner.

Atlas lifted his head. He saw.

“Woof,” said Atlas. Just one.

A little dog heard him through the fence.

“Bark!” cried the little dog.

“Bark bark!” answered another.

The sound leapt from yard to yard until the whole block was barking at once.

Atlas did not bark again. He had already said what he needed to say:

I see you.

The walker smiled and kept going.

One by one, the little dogs ran out of barks.

The street grew quiet.

Atlas lay down. He was still watching.

The big dog knew.
And that was enough.

A living collection

For what children know before the world teaches them not to.

These stories trust quiet perception: the child who belongs to the wild world, the dog who sees before the neighborhood reacts, and the small inner voice that does not need to become loud to become true.
